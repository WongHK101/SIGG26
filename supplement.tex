\section{Protocol Notes and Scope}

This supplement aligns with the current main-paper draft and records additional tables that are useful for reproducibility and reviewer inspection.

The main paper focuses on 10 UAV/UAV-style scenes. This supplement separately reports four additional official aligned scenes whose viewpoints are not part of the UAV-focused main scope. The retained-subset diagnostic for raw unaligned input is reported as an applicability check and should not be interpreted as the main raw full-scene comparison protocol.

Depth diagnostics follow the relative reference-depth protocol. Since raw UAV reconstructions are not in a globally calibrated metric coordinate system, all thresholds are relative-depth ratios rather than metric distances.

\paragraph{Comparison shims.}
UMGS-I-shim and UMGS-J-shim denote UMGS renders bilinearly resized to the external adapter resolution solely for common-mask comparison. They are not distinct training variants and are not used to define the UMGS method.

\paragraph{Grouped split sanitization.}
For official aligned scenes with inconsistent split and transform metadata, we sanitize splits at the capture-group level against the available transforms. A D/G/R/RE/NIR group is removed only when one or more required members are absent from the transform file, making it unusable as a complete multispectral comparison unit. This is a protocol repair for data completeness, not a performance-based selection.

\paragraph{Depth-reference status.}
Official aligned depth diagnostics use sparse point-splat references derived from available transforms and point support. They are secondary reference-depth evidence, not dense MVS or LiDAR ground truth. The main geometry claims therefore use relative-depth consistency and do not claim absolute dense geometry accuracy.

\section{Why Unfrozen-Support Is Not a Product Model}

The Unfrozen-support branch is included as a single-band upper-bound ablation. It starts from the same RGB anchor as UMGS, but allows band-specific geometry and opacity updates in Stage 2. This can improve selected per-band render metrics, but it breaks the shared-support contract required by model-level spectral products.

The failure is concrete in our audit logs. On \texttt{ms\_lake}, Unfrozen-support improves several band PSNR values, but product construction fails with an xyz mismatch between bands:
\[
\max |\Delta xyz| = 6.84\times 10^{-2}.
\]
Scratch-MS training diverges further and triggers Gaussian-count mismatch across bands. These variants can still produce independent band images, and one could compute post-hoc 2D ratios after rendering; however, those ratios are not defined over a common 3D Gaussian support and therefore are not used as valid model-level multispectral products in our protocol.

\section{UMGS-J Initialization Ablation (UMGS-J vs UMGS-JR)}

The main paper keeps UMGS-JR out of the main tables and reports UMGS-J as the representative joint branch. This section records the initialization ablation that motivated that decision. Both variants use the same geometry-locked joint multispectral training design and export per-band evaluation views from a unified checkpoint. They differ only in appearance-bank initialization.

UMGS-J initializes each spectral bank from the reset-appearance state. UMGS-JR initializes each spectral bank from the RGB-tied carrier.

Table~\ref{tab:umgsj-quality-cost} compares these variants on the two joint-branch pilot scenes used during method screening. UMGS-JR does not improve the branch: it degrades image/index quality on both pilot scenes.

\section{Takeaway}

The supplementary tables support three practical points used by the draft: (1) additional official aligned scenes remain available for reproducibility but are outside the main UAV-focused scope, (2) relative-depth trends are stable under threshold sweeps when support is preserved, and (3) UMGS-JR is an initialization ablation rather than a promoted branch.
