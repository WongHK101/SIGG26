\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Evaluation Tracks}

We use two complementary evaluation tracks.

\paragraph{Raw UAV track.}
This track contains three real raw UAV multispectral scenes: \texttt{self\_m3m}, \texttt{external\_vineyard\_A}, and \texttt{external\_vineyard\_B}. It evaluates the full pipeline, including cross-band rectification, QA, RGB anchoring, spectral transfer, product synthesis, and downstream metrics. This track exists primarily to show that the complete pipeline is executable on realistic raw captures.

\paragraph{Official aligned track.}
This track contains three aligned benchmark scenes: \texttt{ms\_lake}, \texttt{ms\_golf}, and \texttt{ms\_solar}. Because alignment is provided, this track removes rectification confound and is the preferred stage for controlled ablations and external baseline comparison.

\subsection{Metrics}

For per-band rendering quality we report SSIM, PSNR, and LPIPS. For multispectral products we report MAE, RMSE, PSNR, and SSIM on NDVI, GNDVI, and NDRE. In the official external comparison, we additionally report common-mask 4-band metrics and paired confidence intervals. The current paper scope emphasizes multispectral product stability and shared-support validity rather than universal dominance on every image metric.

\subsection{External Comparison on Official-Aligned Scenes}

We compare our aligned-scene results against MS-Splatting using a frozen common-mask protocol. For compatibility with the external adapter resolution, we compare against \emph{E3shim}, which bilinearly resizes the render/ground-truth pair of our method to the external adapter resolution. E3shim is a compare shim only, not an additional trained variant.

Two aligned scenes required a protocol repair before external comparison. Their original \texttt{train\_split.json} files were not fully consistent with \texttt{transforms.json}, so we applied grouped split sanitization at the multispectral capture-group level and removed incomplete groups before external training and evaluation.

The external comparison is therefore mixed across scenes rather than uniformly one-sided. This is an important result for how we position the paper. We do not claim universal scene-wise dominance over the external baseline. Instead, we use the official aligned track to show that the proposed pipeline remains competitive under a cleaner protocol while preserving the shared-support structure needed for multispectral synthesis.

\subsection{Ablations}

Our primary official-scene ablations are performed on \texttt{ms\_lake}, which is also the most demanding aligned scene in the external comparison. We test two alternatives:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{geometry unfrozen}: start from the RGB checkpoint but allow geometry and opacity to move during band transfer;
    \item \textbf{from scratch}: train each spectral band independently without RGB-anchored transfer.
\end{itemize}

Both ablations improve some single-band metrics. For example, geometry-unfrozen transfer improves PSNR over the main method by up to $+1.62$ dB on RE and $+1.54$ dB on NIR. The from-scratch variant also improves several individual bands. However, both variants fail at the point that matters most for multispectral reconstruction: they break shared Gaussian support strongly enough that stable products cannot be formed. In our runs, geometry-unfrozen transfer fails with explicit cross-band coordinate mismatch, while from-scratch training fails with large Gaussian-count divergence across bands.

This is the central experimental message of the paper. Image quality alone is insufficient to justify a multispectral method if the representation ceases to support valid cross-band synthesis. The geometry-locked main method is the only variant in our study that simultaneously maintains shared support and completes the full product/index pipeline.

\subsection{Current Scope and Non-Claims}

We intentionally freeze the current paper cycle around the main method and do not continue expansion of alternative multispectral representations. In particular, our exploratory joint multispectral representation variant did not show stable gains over the main method and is therefore not promoted to the main line. We also treat geometry repeatability as a possible appendix-strengthening direction rather than a blocker for the current paper cycle.
