\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Scenes.}
We evaluate the main paper on a 10-scene UAV-focused evaluation suite: 7 raw UAV scenes (UMGS-Field, Vineyard-01--Vineyard-06) and 3 aligned aerial multispectral scenes (Aerial-Golf, Aerial-Lake, Aerial-Solar). Raw scenes test rectification, QA, reconstruction, and products. Aligned scenes remove rectification confounds and are used for external common-mask comparison. Four additional non-UAV official aligned scenes are reported only in the supplement and are not part of the main 10-scene evaluation suite.

\paragraph{Methods.}
We report UMGS-I as the default realization and UMGS-J as a joint band-bank branch of the same geometry-lock contract. MS-Splatting is the external baseline on aligned aerial scenes. Band-only and Unfrozen-support are representative single-band upper-bound ablations; they are not product models because they do not preserve the shared Gaussian support required by model-level multispectral fusion.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Image quality: PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS. Spectral means average G/R/RE/NIR. Index fidelity: MAE/RMSE/PSNR/SSIM for NDVI, GNDVI, NDRE. These image-space metrics are necessary but not sufficient for multispectral products: a method can render plausible held-out views while still producing unstable or incomplete 3D support across weak spectral bands. Cost uses non-invasive GPU traces and exported Gaussian counts. Aligned external comparison uses a common-mask protocol; $^\dagger$ marks UMGS renders resized to the external adapter resolution for comparison only, not distinct trained variants. We therefore report geometry separately as a validation-gated reference-depth diagnostic. Scenes whose reference mesh or camera-frame alignment failed inspection are excluded from depth tables rather than forced into an average.

\subsection{Main Results on UAV Scenes}

The main question is whether locking RGB geometry sacrifices spectral rendering quality. Table~\ref{tab:main-uav10} shows that it does not: UMGS-I and UMGS-J remain near-identical on aggregate image and index quality. On the 10-scene UAV-focused evaluation suite, both reach 0.843 spectral SSIM and 0.844 index SSIM; UMGS-I has a small edge on index RMSE (0.075 vs.\ 0.077). We therefore use UMGS-I as the conservative primary line and treat UMGS-J as a joint-parameterization branch with comparable performance.

\subsection{Reference-Depth Diagnostics}

Table~\ref{tab:validated-depth} reports relative-depth consistency only for reference sources that passed visual and protocol validation. The diagnostic is not an absolute geometry benchmark: it evaluates agreement with a scene-specific reference under held-out probe cameras. This table is where the difference between appearance scores and geometry stability becomes clear. On the four validated scenes, UMGS-I and UMGS-J have much lower relative depth error and higher agreement rates than the retained MS-Splatting adapter: AbsRel is $0.023$ vs.\ $0.195$ (about $8.5\times$ lower), Ag@5\% is $0.867$ vs.\ $0.526$, and Ag@10\% is $0.922$ vs.\ $0.610$. This is a substantially stronger separation than the aligned PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS comparison, and it directly targets the geometry stability needed for spectral-index products. Per-scene breakdowns in the supplement show that the AbsRel separation is present on all four validated scenes, not caused by a single outlier. Band-only has a low mean AbsRel only on the small fraction where it yields valid depth (Valid $0.410$), so it should not be read as a better geometry model. Unfrozen-support can be close in depth consistency, but it fails the shared-support/product contract and is therefore not a valid spectral product model. Figure~\ref{fig:depth-umgs-field} visualizes the same diagnostic on our project-owned UMGS-Field capture: UMGS-I/J maintain broad, coherent depth support, while retained MS-Splatting shows larger local deviations. Scene-level depth breakdowns and all-band visual grids are provided in the supplement.

\subsection{Aligned External Comparison}

Aligned scenes remove the raw-rectification factor and isolate the reconstruction model. Under this common-mask setting, Table~\ref{tab:mms-official-uav3} shows competitive, scene-dependent image-space behavior rather than universal dominance: UMGS has lower LPIPS, matches mean SSIM/SAM, and remains within a small margin in PSNR and spectral RMSE. Scene-level results in Table~\ref{tab:mms-scenes} are also mixed. This is precisely why we do not use the classic 3DGS image metrics as the sole evidence. They establish that UMGS remains competitive in aligned rendering, but they do not measure whether the learned splats form stable geometry for multispectral products. The stronger distinction appears in product readiness, shared-support validity, and the validation-gated reference-depth consistency in Table~\ref{tab:validated-depth}.

\subsection{Shared-Support Contract Ablation}

This ablation tests whether geometry locking is merely conservative or actually necessary. Table~\ref{tab:ablations3} uses the same four representative scenes as the reference-depth diagnostic: UMGS-Field, Vineyard-01, Vineyard-03, and Aerial-Golf. Band-only and Unfrozen-support can be useful single-band diagnostic branches, but they are not valid product models. The audit logs record Gaussian-count/identity mismatch for Band-only and band-specific xyz drift for Unfrozen-support (up to $6.61\times10^{-2}$ in the current representative audits). Quantitative audit logs in the supplement report exact support equality for both UMGS-I and UMGS-J on the representative audited outputs: UMGS-I passes direct per-band support audits across four scenes and 16 spectral rows, while UMGS-J passes the direct joint-checkpoint invariant audit. These failures occur when multispectral products are assembled, showing that per-band fitting alone is insufficient if support consistency is lost.

\subsection{Raw-Conditioning Focused Ablation}

To isolate the role of valid-region supervision in raw cross-FOV captures, we run focused no-valid-mask ablations on three raw UAV scenes: UMGS-Field, Vineyard-03, and Vineyard-01. In each case, we keep the geometry-lock protocol unchanged and disable validity masking during spectral supervision only. Training audits confirm that validity masking was disabled and no masked-loss steps were observed for all spectral bands. Support audits for the ablation outputs show zero support drift across bands, confirming that the degradation is caused by removing valid-region supervision rather than by breaking the geometry-lock contract. Under the same held-out views and evaluation coverage, removing valid-region supervision consistently degrades vegetation-index fidelity: all 9 scene-index rows show higher RMSE and lower SSIM. Averaged over the three scenes and three indices, RMSE increases by about 40.2\% and SSIM decreases by about 0.0294. These focused ablations support valid-region supervision as an important component for raw UAV multispectral product synthesis.
