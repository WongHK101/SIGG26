UAV multispectral reconstruction is attractive for agricultural and environmental monitoring, but it creates a structural mismatch for Gaussian splatting pipelines: low-texture bands are weak geometry cues, while downstream spectral products and vegetation indices require stable cross-band correspondence. We address this problem with an RGB-anchored, geometry-locked multispectral Gaussian pipeline. The method first establishes a shared Gaussian support from RGB, then learns band-specific appearance transfer for G/R/RE/NIR while keeping the shared geometry fixed. For raw UAV captures, we pair the pipeline with a rectification and quality-audit stage that explicitly distinguishes strict pass, warning, and fail cases under a baseline-aware protocol. 

We evaluate the pipeline on three raw UAV scenes and three official aligned multispectral scenes. On the aligned benchmark, our method reaches mean SSIM scores of 0.7982/0.7791/0.7861 for NDVI/GNDVI/NDRE and supports a complete reviewer-facing comparison against an external multispectral Gaussian baseline. That comparison is mixed across scenes rather than uniformly one-sided: one scene favors the external baseline, one favors our method, and one is mixed while the joint 4-band metrics favor our method. We therefore argue for robustness of shared-support multispectral synthesis rather than universal dominance on every scene. Ablations further show that geometry-unfrozen and from-scratch alternatives can improve some single-band metrics, but break the shared Gaussian support needed for stable multispectral products. Together, these results support geometry-locked multispectral Gaussian reconstruction as a practical formulation for UAV multispectral imaging.
