UAV multispectral reconstruction couples a rendering problem with a geometric registration problem. Non-RGB bands such as G/R/RE/NIR provide weaker texture and less reliable local features than RGB, while practical UAV rigs introduce cross-FOV, resolution, and alignment differences across cameras. These effects make direct spectral reconstruction fragile: even when individual bands render well, small support drift can bias ratio-based products such as NDVI, GNDVI, and NDRE.

We propose UMGS, a two-stage UAV multispectral Gaussian splatting framework for spectral-index synthesis. UMGS first reconstructs an RGB geometric anchor, then transfers G/R/RE/NIR appearance while freezing Gaussian positions, covariances, opacity, and identity. This geometry lock turns the RGB reconstruction into a stable carrier for low-texture spectral bands. We instantiate the idea as UMGS-I, with independent per-band transfer, and UMGS-J, with joint band-bank transfer in a unified multispectral checkpoint.

We evaluate on a 10-scene UAV-focused benchmark with 7 raw UAV captures and 3 aligned aerial multispectral scenes. UMGS-I and UMGS-J are numerically near-tied and show matched relative-depth behavior under held-out probe views. On aligned aerial scenes, common-mask comparison against MS-Splatting is competitive and scene-dependent. Ablations show why locking is necessary: Scratch-MS and Unfrozen-support can improve selected single-band metrics, but they break the shared support required for model-level spectral-index synthesis.

These results support RGB-anchored geometry locking as a practical design for stable UAV multispectral Gaussian splatting.
