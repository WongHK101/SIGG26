UAV multispectral reconstruction is not only a rendering problem; it is also a shared-support problem. Multispectral bands are weak geometry drivers, with lower texture and less reliable local features than RGB, while UAV rigs introduce cross-FOV, resolution, and alignment differences across cameras. Direct spectral reconstruction can therefore be unreliable: independently reconstructed bands may drift in geometry and support, preventing stable 3D spectral-index synthesis. We introduce UMGS, a two-stage 3D Gaussian Splatting framework that first reconstructs an RGB Gaussian anchor and then learns multispectral appearance with Gaussian positions, covariances, opacity, and identity frozen. This geometry lock lets low-texture spectral bands inherit stable 3D support while retaining band-specific appearance. UMGS-I is our primary independent-transfer realization, and UMGS-J is a close joint band-bank counterpart. Experiments on raw UAV and aligned aerial multispectral scenes show that UMGS preserves shared 3D support and remains competitive with multispectral Gaussian baselines under aligned common-mask comparison.
