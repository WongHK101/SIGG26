Multispectral UAV reconstruction is not only a rendering task; it is a shared-support problem. A usable model must preserve per-band image quality and keep G/R/RE/NIR predictions on the same geometric support so that downstream indices (e.g., NDVI, GNDVI, NDRE) remain stable. Existing Gaussian pipelines can improve single-band metrics while still drifting across bands, which makes product synthesis unreliable.

We propose UMGS, a geometry-locked UAV multispectral Gaussian splatting framework for spectral-index synthesis. Stage 1 learns an RGB anchor with standard Gaussian splatting. Stage 2 transfers spectral appearance while freezing geometry, opacity, and Gaussian identity. We study two locked variants: UMGS-I, independent per-band transfer from the shared RGB anchor, and UMGS-J, joint band-bank transfer in one unified multispectral state with a single authoritative checkpoint and per-band exported evaluation views. For raw UAV captures, we introduce a baseline-aware rectification QA protocol that distinguishes strict pass, pass-with-warning, and fail, preventing weak initialization baselines from incorrectly rejecting otherwise stable cross-modal alignment.

We evaluate on 14 scenes (7 raw UAV, 7 official aligned). UMGS-I and UMGS-J are numerically close on the full suite (4-band mean SSIM 0.820 for both; mean index SSIM 0.806 for both) and show matched relative-depth geometry behavior under held-out probe views. On official aligned scenes, common-mask comparison against MS-Splatting is competitive and scene-dependent; on the 7-scene mean, our shims are better in SSIM, LPIPS, spectral RMSE, and SAM. Ablations confirm the role of geometry locking: Scratch-MS and Unfrozen-support can improve selected single-band metrics, but they break the shared Gaussian support needed for model-level multispectral products and index synthesis.

Overall, the results support RGB-anchored geometry locking as a reliable design for practical UAV multispectral Gaussian reconstruction.
