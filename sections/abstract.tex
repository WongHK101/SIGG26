Multispectral UAV reconstruction is not only a rendering task; it is a shared-support problem. A usable model must preserve per-band image quality and keep G/R/RE/NIR predictions on the same geometric support so that downstream indices (e.g., NDVI, GNDVI, NDRE) remain stable. Existing Gaussian pipelines can improve single-band metrics while still drifting across bands, which makes product synthesis unreliable.

We propose a geometry-locked multispectral Gaussian pipeline. Stage 1 learns an RGB anchor with standard Gaussian splatting. Stage 2 transfers spectral appearance while freezing geometry, opacity, and Gaussian identity. We study two locked variants: E3 (independent per-band transfer from the shared RGB anchor) and E4b-zero (joint band-bank transfer in one unified multispectral state with a single authoritative checkpoint and per-band exported evaluation views). For raw UAV captures, we introduce a baseline-aware rectification QA protocol that distinguishes strict pass, pass-with-warning, and fail, preventing weak initialization baselines from incorrectly rejecting otherwise stable cross-modal alignment.

We evaluate on 14 scenes (7 raw UAV, 7 official aligned). E3 and E4b-zero are numerically close on the full suite (4-band mean SSIM 0.820 for both; mean index SSIM 0.806 for both) and show matched relative-depth geometry behavior under held-out probe views. On official aligned scenes, common-mask comparison against MS-Splatting is competitive and scene-dependent; on the 7-scene mean, our shims are better in SSIM, LPIPS, spectral RMSE, and SAM. Ablations confirm the role of geometry locking: from-scratch and geometry-unfrozen variants may improve selected image metrics but weaken depth agreement or violate shared-support assumptions required by multispectral products.

Overall, the results support RGB-anchored geometry locking as a reliable design for practical UAV multispectral Gaussian reconstruction.
