\section{Conclusion}

We presented an RGB-anchored, geometry-locked multispectral Gaussian pipeline for UAV imaging. The key decision in this work is to treat stable shared support as a primary objective rather than an incidental byproduct of band-wise rendering. Across raw UAV scenes, aligned benchmark scenes, official-scene ablations, and an external comparison against MS-Splatting, the same pattern recurs: methods that relax geometry constraints can improve some image metrics, but they also break the cross-band consistency required by multispectral product synthesis. Our main method remains competitive on the aligned benchmark while being the only configuration in our study that consistently preserves valid multispectral support.

The most realistic next step is to strengthen the geometry story further with a dedicated repeatability or self-consistency evaluator, rather than to enlarge the current set of rendering baselines. For the current paper cycle, however, the evidence already supports geometry-locked multispectral Gaussian reconstruction as a practical and defensible design point for UAV multispectral imaging.
